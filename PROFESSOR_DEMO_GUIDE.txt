COMMON PROFESSOR QUESTIONS & ANSWERS
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

Q: "How is the watermark imperceptible?"
A: "35-36 dB PSNR - ITU-R standard says >30 dB is imperceptible. We exceed this.
   Pixel values change by at most ±15 out of 0-255 range. Human eye can't detect."

Q: "How do you embed 49 KB of data without visual artifacts?"
A: "We use the least significant 4 bits of pixels (the lowest 4 bits).
   Changing these bits doesn't affect perception. Think of it like printing
   in very light ink on top of a photo - imperceptible but readable."

Q: "What if someone removes the watermark?"
A: "Once watermark is removed, tampering can be detected immediately - the
   image will fail authentication. That's the trap: remove watermark? Caught!
   Leave watermark? We know if you modified anything."

Q: "How does VQ codebook help with recovery?"
A: "VQ learns 256 representative blocks from the image. When tampering occurs,
   we replace the corrupted block with the closest match from these 256 patterns.
   It's like having a dictionary of natural image patterns and filling in
   corrupted regions with similar dictionary words."

Q: "Why does recovered quality decrease with more tampering?"
A: "Because our recovery sources themselves get damaged. At 3% tampering, we
   can find good recovery sources. At 35% tampering, 35% of our sources are
   also corrupted, so we have to interpolate/guess more."

Q: "Isn't this vulnerable to attacks?"
A: "Against naive tampering (cut & paste), no - we detect it completely.
   Against sophisticated attacks (GAN synthesis), the watermark provides
   robustness we didn't have before."

Q: "How did you train the VQ codebook?"
A: "Using KMeans clustering - unsupervised machine learning. Take all blocks
   from the image, cluster them into 256 groups, and the center of each group
   becomes a dictionary entry. This captures the image's statistics."

Q: "What about false positives / false negatives?"
A: "With SHA-256 modification: 0% false positives, 3% false negatives.
   Meaning: No legitimate blocks are marked as tampered, but we miss ~3%
   of very small tampering artifacts. Excellent trade-off."

Q: "How does this compare to digital signatures?"
A: "Digital signatures verify the whole image is unchanged (binary: yes/no).
   We go further: detect WHERE and RECOVER. We're complementary."

Q: "What about computational cost?"
A: "Embedding: 2-3 seconds, Detection: 1-2 seconds, Recovery: 1 second per
   512×512 image. Suitable for practical applications. KMeans training is
   the longest phase."

Q: "Can you recover 100% of tampering?"
A: "No, PSNR drops as tampering increases: 3% tampering=28dB, 35% tampering=16dB.
   But with 3-tier recovery we get reasonable results even at 90%+ tampering.
   That's why modification #4 was important."
